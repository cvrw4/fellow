# Fellow meeting page — design challenge spec

**Role:** Product design  
**Surface:** Desktop meeting page  
**Timebox:** About half a day of focused work; **1 week** to submit (due **Monday, October 19, 2026**)  
**Review:** Async by the hiring manager, then a discussion call if it goes well  
**Starter file:** [Fellow Design Challenge](https://www.figma.com/design/Vdb5WMOZ5B2P4DuFZrs4am/Fellow-Design-Challenge?node-id=0-1&t=ts5l2F58qLRihIEN-1)

Reference screens for this brief:

- Fellow’s current meeting page (dense, multiplayer, three columns)
- Granola’s meeting page (quiet, single-player, one writing surface)

---

## 1. What this challenge is for

Product sense and craft both have to be visible. The work is a meeting-page redesign that shows how you think through a problem that is both product and visual: what to change, what to keep, and how simplicity, capability, and quality stay in balance.

Fellow’s ambition for the next year is to sit at the same design level as **Notion** and **Figma**: a tool people trust for serious work, with an interface that feels inevitable, quiet, and precise.

---

## 2. Problem

The meeting-recording market covers a wide range of needs. Fellow wins partly because of multiplayer collaboration and a broader platform. That breadth also produces a denser interface: more features, more affordances, more visual complexity.

Customers often arrive after outgrowing simpler, consumer-grade tools such as Granola. Granola is a focused, single-player product. Its interface is easier to adopt and sets a higher bar for visual polish. That polish becomes the quality expectation people bring with them.

Two pressures follow:

1. Reduce the **perceived complexity** of Fellow’s meeting page.
2. Raise the **visual design** of the page to a world-class level.

The platform’s power stays. The experience should feel simpler, more cohesive, and exacting in its execution.

---

## 3. The job of the redesign

Redesign Fellow’s **meeting page** so it is easier to adopt and visually world-class, while the collaborative meeting platform remains fully available.

Everything on the page is open: layout, interaction, typography, color, borders, padding, iconography, hierarchy, and disclosure.

Questions the work should be able to answer:

- What makes the visual design feel world-class?
- How is information and action hierarchy clearer?
- What is progressively disclosed, and what stays always visible?
- What is visually noisy, repetitive, or more prominent than its job requires?
- What makes the page feel more intuitive and cohesive?

Direction and depth are open. The judgment matters as much as the pixels: what changes, what stays, and why.

---

## 4. North star

Design toward the standard Notion and Figma set for tools people live in every day.

| Quality | What it looks like on this page |
| --- | --- |
| Quiet canvas | The meeting itself is the product. Chrome recedes until it is needed. |
| One clear job at a time | Before, during, and after a meeting each have a primary action. Secondary actions wait. |
| Precise type and space | A small type scale, consistent rhythm, and padding that feels intentional at every size. |
| Restrained color | Neutrals carry the layout. Color marks state, selection, and the one primary action. |
| Honest hierarchy | Titles, metadata, content, and controls are visually distinct without extra boxes, rules, or icons. |
| Power on demand | Collaboration, AI, recording, and meeting states are one gesture away, and stay out of the way until then. |
| Cohesion | One visual language across the calendar, the note, and Ask Fellow. Shared radii, strokes, type, and motion. |

Granola is the adoption and polish bar, not the product model. Fellow stays multiplayer. The page should feel as calm as a focused tool and as capable as a platform.

## 5. What is on the page today

Inventory from the current Fellow meeting page. These capabilities stay in the product. They can move, combine, restyle, or hide until needed. They are not removed.

### App chrome

- Workspace switcher (Acme Co)
- Back / forward
- Favorite
- Notifications
- Search
- Ask Fellow entry point

### Left navigation

- Calendar (current)
- Library
- Action Items
- More

Library and Action Items are neighboring destinations. Their pages are out of scope. The nav can be restyled as part of the meeting-page frame.

### Calendar column

- Date (Wed, May 8) and add-meeting
- Today / week view
- Agenda grouped by day (Wednesday 8, Thursday 9, Friday 10)
- Current-time marker (2:16 PM)
- Meeting row states:
  - Past meeting with recap
  - Now and recording
  - Selected meeting
  - Unread changes
  - Will be recorded
  - Declined
  - Cancelled
  - Not accepted

### Meeting header

- Title: Project Phoenix Weekly
- Favorite
- When: Today, 2:30–3:00 PM (header chip reads 2:00–2:30 PM on the selected row’s neighbor — preserve a clear, single time treatment)
- Video provider: Google Meet
- Participants
- Share
- Copy link
- More actions

### Meeting body

- Follow-ups from last meeting
- **Talking points** — “The things to talk about,” add a talking point
- **Action items** — “What came out of this meeting? What are your next steps?,” add an action item
- **Notepad** — “Anything else to write down?”
- **Previous meetings in this series** — recap cards with title, relative time, summary, and thumbnail (example: yesterday; Feb 8)

### Ask Fellow (right panel)

- Panel open / history / close
- Shortcuts: Prep for this meeting, Icebreakers, Previous Meeting Open Action Items, custom (“My first shortcut”)
- Meeting context chip (`@Project Phoenix Weekly`)
- Composer: prompt, attach, slash commands, send
- Example prompt: “Did we resolve the open questions?”

### System status

- Auto-record on (toast)
- Live recording indicator on the in-progress meeting

### Granola, as the contrast

Granola’s meeting page is a near-empty note:

- Title, date, “Me,” copy
- A single “Write notes” field
- A bottom bar: Resume, Ask anything, Write follow-up email
- One primary button: Generate notes

That emptiness is the feeling to learn from. Fellow’s extra structure — agenda, collaboration, series history, recording, shared action items — is the capability to keep.

## 6. Constraints

**In scope**

- The desktop meeting page and the chrome required to use it
- Layout, interaction, type, color, borders, spacing, iconography
- Hierarchy and progressive disclosure
- Product color, if a change serves the visual system (the logo stays)

**Out of scope**

- Pages outside meetings: Library, Action Items, and the rest of the app
- Logo redesign
- Mobile, browser extension, and other platforms
- Removing a capability that exists on the page

**Process**

- Spend as long as is useful; the expected effort is no more than half a day
- Submit within one week
- Be ready to walk through the interpretation, the factors, the assumptions, and the reasoning

## 7. How the work will be judged

**Product sense.** How the problem was broken down. The hypotheses, the changes, and the justification. What was preserved, and why that preservation is a design decision.

**Visual design.** Whether the execution is world-class when limitations are minimal. Type, color, space, alignment, states, and restraint should withstand a close look.

## 9. Working hypotheses

These are starting assumptions to test in the design, not conclusions.

1. **The note is the canvas.** Talking points, action items, and the notepad compete as three equal sections. A single writing surface, with those objects inside it, would make the page feel closer to Granola without dropping structure.
2. **The calendar is a navigator, not a second product.** Row states (recording, unread, declined, cancelled, not accepted, recap) are all useful and currently each have their own visual treatment. A shared row with one status treatment would cut noise.
3. **Ask Fellow is a mode, not a permanent column.** A full-time third column makes every meeting feel like a tool with a tool beside it. Summoning it from the composer, the way Granola docks “Ask anything,” would return width to the note.
4. **Series history and follow-ups are context, not peers of the agenda.** They matter before and after the meeting. During the meeting they can sit behind a disclosure.
5. **One accent, one primary action per moment.** Prep before, capture during, generate or share after. Recording state, selection, and AI can share a disciplined color system instead of red dots, blue selection, green time, and purple Ask Fellow all speaking at once.
6. **Collaboration stays visible but light.** Participants, share, and unread changes are the multiplayer signal. They belong in the header, always available, never decorated.

---

## 10. Craft bar

Judge the file the way a Notion or Figma design would be judged:

- Alignment on a consistent grid; optical alignment where the grid is wrong
- A type scale of a few sizes, with weight doing hierarchy work
- Spacing from one scale, including the space inside rows, cards, and the composer
- Borders and dividers used rarely; separation by space first
- Icons at one size and stroke, only where they disambiguate
- Hover, pressed, selected, disabled, and empty states designed, not implied
- Radii, shadows, and color from a small set of tokens
- Text that can wrap, and a layout that still holds when the agenda is long or the title is long
